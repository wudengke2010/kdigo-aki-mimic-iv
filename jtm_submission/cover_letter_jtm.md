**Re:** Submission of original research article — "KDIGO Creatinine Staging and Transient Versus Persistent AKI Phenotypes Are Independently Associated with 30-Day Mortality in Critically Ill Patients: A Dual-Cohort Study with Locked-Model External Validation (MIMIC-IV v3.1 and eICU-CRD)"

Dear Editor,

On behalf of all authors, I am pleased to submit our manuscript for consideration as an original research article in the *Journal of Translational Medicine*.

**What our study adds.** AKI staging is universally applied at the bedside, yet how it is implemented in large clinical databases frequently deviates from the KDIGO specification — temporal windows are ignored, entire-admission creatinine values are used, and severity scores that embed renal function are used for adjustment. In 173,972 critically ill patients across two independent databases (MIMIC-IV v3.1, derivation; eICU-CRD, external validation), we show that KDIGO staging implemented *exactly as specified* retains a graded, monotonic association with 30-day mortality after conservative (non-renal SOFA) severity adjustment, with Stage 3 hazard ratios of 2.45 (95% CI 2.26–2.66) and 2.46 (2.31–2.63) in the two cohorts — a cross-cohort ratio of 1.00.

**The most clinically actionable finding** is that AKI *duration* outperforms *peak stage*: AKI resolving within 72 hours of onset carried a near-null adjusted mortality hazard (HR 1.07, 0.99–1.15 in MIMIC-IV; 1.24, 1.16–1.32 in eICU-CRD), whereas AKI persisting beyond 72 hours carried a 2.63- to 3.24-fold hazard, and a simple transient/persistent phenotype model discriminated better than KDIGO stage itself (C-index 0.768 vs 0.753; 0.789 vs 0.716). A creatinine value that meets Stage 1 criteria but resolves by day 3 is prognostically closer to no AKI than to Stage 3 — a distinction directly actionable in bedside risk assessment and one that peak-stage reporting obscures.

**Differentiation from the closest prior work.** Lin et al. (Clin Kidney J 2025) identified AKI subphenotypes in 17,113 patients using multivariate group-based trajectory modelling on MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD. Our work differs in three substantive ways: (1) **scale** — 173,972 patients, roughly ten-fold larger; (2) **clinical deployability** — a dichotomous, bedside-applicable 72-hour resolution criterion requiring no trajectory modelling or software; and (3) **validation rigour** — our fully adjusted model was *locked* and transported to eICU-CRD without any refitting (TRIPOD spirit), quantifying both what transports (Stage 3 hazard, age, SOFA weights; calibration slope 0.96) and what does not (mechanical ventilation reversed direction between cohorts, which we report transparently). To our knowledge, this is among the first KDIGO staging studies with genuine locked-model transportability assessment across independent critical care databases.

**Fit with the Journal.** The study addresses a data-driven clinical decision problem of direct translational relevance: it quantifies how implementation choices in routinely collected data drive apparent AKI epidemiology, provides transportable prognostic weights, and delivers a phenotype refinement that improves risk stratification using information clinicians already have. All analyses are reported per STROBE; the external validation follows the TRIPOD framework. The full analysis pipeline is publicly available, supporting reproducibility.
