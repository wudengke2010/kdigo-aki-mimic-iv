# Cover Letter

**To:** The Editorial Board, Clinical Kidney Journal

**Date:** August 8, 2026

**Re:** Submission of Original Article — "Association of KDIGO Creatinine-Based AKI Staging with ICU Mortality: A Dual-Cohort Retrospective Study Using MIMIC-IV v3.1 and eICU-CRD"

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our original research article for consideration by Clinical Kidney Journal. This study addresses four critical knowledge gaps in KDIGO AKI staging using a dual-cohort design with 250,540 ICU stays from two independent databases.

## What This Study Adds

Despite the widespread adoption of KDIGO staging, several fundamental questions remain unresolved: (1) the degree to which illness severity confounds the KDIGO–mortality association has not been quantified with formal reclassification metrics; (2) the Stage 2–3 mortality convergence lacks a time-resolved explanation; (3) the "AKI paradox" (where CKD patients tolerate severe AKI better than non-CKD patients) has not been validated across independent databases; and (4) no KDIGO-based clinical prediction tool has been externally validated.

Our study directly addresses all four gaps: we quantified that illness severity accounts for 32–59% of the KDIGO–mortality association (NRI = 0.625), revealed through time-stratified Cox analysis that Stage 3's hazard progressively increases over follow-up (HR 1.41→1.86), replicated the AKI paradox in an independent multi-centre cohort (eICU-CRD, interaction OR 0.726, p < 0.001), and deployed a five-variable nomogram with web-based calculator (AUC = 0.818).

## Differentiation from Lin et al. (CKJ 2025)

We note that Lin et al. [25] published a MIMIC-IV + eICU-CRD dual-cohort AKI study in CKJ (2025;18(6):sfaf142), using trajectory-based modelling to identify AKI subphenotypes in 17,113 patients. Our study uses the same two databases but addresses a fundamentally different question: KDIGO staging validation and AKI paradox decomposition, rather than subphenotype identification. Our sample size (250,540) is 15-fold larger, and we employ different analytical frameworks (incremental logistic regression with NRI/IDI reclassification, time-stratified Cox, formal CKD × KDIGO interaction testing, E-values, nomogram). These complementary approaches—trajectory subphenotyping vs. staging validation—provide distinct insights into AKI prognosis.

## Novelty and Significance

To our knowledge, this is the first study to simultaneously:
1. Quantify illness severity confounding of KDIGO staging with NRI/IDI reclassification metrics in a cohort exceeding 80,000 patients
2. Replicate the AKI paradox across independent databases with formal interaction testing
3. Assess time-dependent predictive performance of KDIGO staging (7/14/28-day ROC)
4. Deploy a KDIGO-integrated nomogram with web calculator validated in an external cohort

The Gleeson et al. [29] muscle mass mechanism provides a biological explanation for the AKI paradox: creatinine is a muscle-derived marker, so elevated creatinine in non-CKD patients with preserved muscle mass may reflect less catastrophic injury than the same level in sarcopenic patients. Our cross-database replication (MIMIC OR 0.476; eICU OR 0.726) supports this as a biological phenomenon rather than a single-cohort artefact.

## CKJ Relevance

This study is directly relevant to CKJ's scope of clinical kidney research. All 9 CKJ-published references [20,24–32] demonstrate the journal's active interest in AKI staging, validation, prediction tools, and database studies. Our findings have immediate clinical implications: the web calculator enables real-time mortality risk estimation at the bedside using five routinely available ICU variables.
